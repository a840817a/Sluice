# Decrypted DASH AdaptationSets are rewritten in place

When a channel holds a content key for every Representation of a DASH AdaptationSet encrypted with `cenc` or `cbcs`, Sluice decrypts its segments during ingest instead of passing ciphertext through, which is the one exception to storing segments as the origin produced them. Samples are decrypted where they lie in `mdat`, and the boxes in `moof` that only describe the encryption (`senc` and its PIFF form, `saiz`, `saio`, `pssh`) keep their size but are retyped to `free`; the init segment is rewritten as a clear one (the encrypted sample entry takes its `frma` format, `sinf` and `pssh` are dropped). We chose this over remuxing because a segment then keeps its length and every box its offset, so trun data offsets, `sidx` entries and SegmentBase byte ranges stay valid with nothing recomputed. A wrong key cannot be detected: AES-CTR and AES-CBC decrypt any bytes without error, so a mistyped key yields unplayable segments, not a failure.
